# Spoiler Policy

## Purpose

Define spoiler-handling rules for all Film project conversations,
recommendations, reactions, event generation, and source documents.

Spoiler prevention is a core runtime requirement of this project.

The default assumption should always be:

```text
The user has not seen the movie or series.
```

## Core Principles

- Avoid spoilers unless the user explicitly requests them.
- Prefer ambiguity over accidental revelation.
- Preserve discovery, surprise, tension, and emotional payoff.
- Do not reveal twists indirectly through implication.
- Do not assume older media is safe to spoil.
- Do not assume cultural popularity removes spoiler sensitivity.
- Avoid trailer-style spoiler behavior.
- Avoid “safe spoilers.”
- When uncertain, reveal less.

## Forbidden Content

Do not reveal:

- endings
- plot twists
- hidden identities
- surprise appearances
- deaths
- betrayals
- relationship outcomes
- final outcomes
- season-ending events
- cliffhangers
- future-season developments
- tonal shifts tied to plot revelations
- major character arcs
- mystery solutions
- structural surprises
- timeline manipulation details
- “you won’t believe what happens” style hints

## Indirect Spoilers

Avoid indirect spoilers such as:

- “the ending changes everything”
- “stick with it until episode 6”
- “a major character dies”
- “the villain is actually…”
- “season 2 recontextualizes season 1”
- “the show becomes much darker later”
- “don’t get attached to anyone”

Even vague framing can reduce surprise.

## Recommendation Rules

Recommendations should focus on:

- tone
- atmosphere
- pacing
- genre
- structure
- acting quality
- cinematography
- emotional style
- thematic similarity
- viewer commitment expectations

Preferred phrasing:

- “Feels similar to X.”
- “You may like this because you liked Y.”
- “Slow-burn political thriller.”
- “Character-focused crime drama.”
- “Strong atmosphere and tension.”

Avoid:

- plot setup explanations beyond high-level premises
- twist framing
- narrative trajectory hints
- payoff descriptions

## Allowed High-Level Premises

A short spoiler-free premise summary is allowed when useful.

The summary should resemble:

- a minimal IMDb description
- early public setup information
- genre-level framing

Allowed example:

```text
A historical epic about a Scottish warrior resisting English rule.
```

Not allowed:

```text
A man seeks revenge after a devastating personal tragedy.
```

The second example implies emotional and structural outcomes.

## Mid-Series Guidance

When discussing unfinished series:

- avoid future-episode hints
- avoid “it gets better” specifics
- avoid describing future tonal changes in detail
- avoid discussing future cast changes
- avoid describing future pacing improvements precisely

Preferred:

```text
If the current pacing and tone are already frustrating you, the series
may not be a good fit.
```

Avoid:

```text
Season 3 fixes all the problems.
```

## User-Requested Spoilers

Spoilers are allowed only when the user explicitly requests them.

The request should be clear and intentional.

Examples:

- “Spoil it for me.”
- “What happens?”
- “Tell me the ending.”
- “I do not care about spoilers.”

Even then:

- reveal only what was requested
- avoid expanding into unrelated spoilers
- avoid spoiling adjacent media unnecessarily

## Event and Data Rules

Events and generated source documents should remain spoiler-free.

Avoid recording spoilers in:

- reactions
- notes
- recommendation signals
- summaries
- generated preference profiles

Prefer:

- tone
- pacing
- atmosphere
- genre descriptors
- emotional reactions
- structural observations

Avoid:

- twist descriptions
- ending summaries
- outcome descriptions
- future-season revelations

## Trailer Philosophy

Assume the user prefers less information than typical movie marketing.

Do not emulate:

- trailers
- teaser breakdowns
- marketing hype
- “best moments” summaries
- spoiler-heavy fan discussions

The project should optimize for discovery and first-time viewing
experience preservation.

## Recommendation Philosophy

The goal is not merely accurate recommendations.

The goal is:

- preserving excitement
- preserving curiosity
- preserving emotional payoff
- protecting first-view experiences
- helping the user spend limited viewing time wisely

When forced to choose between:

- persuasive detail
- spoiler safety

prefer spoiler safety.
